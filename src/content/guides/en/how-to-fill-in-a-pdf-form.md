Tax forms, applications, registration sheets and contracts often arrive as PDFs with boxes to fill. The answers on them are personal: names, addresses, numbers. This guide shows how to fill a form on your own device so nothing you type is uploaded, what to do when a form will not let you type and how to decide whether to lock the answers afterwards.

## How to fill a form with LeafLock

Your answers are written into the PDF by your browser on your device. Nothing you type is uploaded.

1. Open [Fill PDF form](/fill-pdf-form) and choose the form.
2. Type into the fields: text boxes, check boxes, drop-downs and the like.
3. Choose whether to lock the answers in.
4. Save and download the filled-in copy.

## "This PDF has no fillable fields"

Many forms are just pictures of a form, or documents with lines drawn on them. They have no interactive fields, which is common for scanned or printed forms. In that case, use [Edit PDF](/edit-pdf) to type on top of the page instead, placing text boxes over the lines.

## Locking the answers

**Locking the answers in** turns the fields into ordinary page content, so the answers can no longer be changed in a PDF reader. Choose it for a final copy you are sending or archiving. Leave it off when you or someone else needs to edit the answers later, for example a form that another person must complete next.

You can also lock a form on its own later with [Flatten PDF](/flatten-pdf); see [how to sign a PDF without printing](/guides/how-to-sign-a-pdf-without-printing), which explains where flattening fits in a signing flow.

## Dynamic (XFA) forms

Some forms exist only as XFA, an older dynamic format. Those cannot be filled here. If a form also has a standard version inside it, that version is filled and kept. If a form fails to show fields, check whether the issuer offers a standard PDF alternative.

## Answers in other languages

You can type answers in other languages, and PDF readers will show them. But answers using characters outside the Latin alphabet cannot be locked in yet, so save those without locking.

## Signature fields

Signature fields cannot be filled in here. Save the filled form, then use [Sign PDF](/sign-pdf) to place your signature on it.

## Before you send

1. Open the saved file and check each page. Make sure every required field is filled in.
2. Check numbers, dates and names twice, particularly ID and account numbers.
3. Look at check boxes and drop-downs, which are easy to skip.
4. If the form should not be altered afterwards, lock the answers.
5. Consider whether the file's properties reveal more than you intend; see [Remove metadata](/remove-metadata).

## Repeating forms

If you fill the same details into many forms, such as your address, keep a blank copy of each form and fill in a new copy every time. Your original blank stays reusable because saving always makes a new file.

## Privacy

Forms ask for exactly the kind of data you would not want to send to a stranger's server: identification numbers, income, health details. Filling on your device means they stay on it until you decide to send the finished file.

## Summary

1. Fill in the fields; the answers never leave your device.
2. No fillable fields? Type on top with Edit PDF.
3. Lock the answers for final copies; leave them editable otherwise.
4. XFA-only forms and signature fields need other routes.
