**Short answer:** [RTF to PDF](/rtf-to-pdf) keeps paragraphs, bold and italic text on plain A4 pages in a single typeface. Headings, lists, tables, images, fonts and colors do not come across, so use [Word to PDF](/word-to-pdf) if you need headings and lists.

Rich Text Format is an old, widely supported document format. Plenty of programs still save it: older word processors, note apps, templates exported from other systems and legal software. Converting an .rtf to PDF is straightforward, but this tool is narrower than some others, and knowing where its edges are saves a surprise. This guide covers what it keeps, what it leaves out and what to do instead when you need more.

## What comes across

[RTF to PDF](/rtf-to-pdf) keeps:

- Paragraphs
- Bold text
- Italic text

all laid out on plain A4 pages in a single typeface.

## What does not come across

- Headings
- Lists
- Tables
- Images
- Fonts and colors

The reason is specific. RTF has no reliable way to tell a heading from a styled paragraph without reading the document's full style sheet. Rather than guess, every paragraph is treated the same way. That is a narrower scope than [Word to PDF](/word-to-pdf), and it is stated plainly because guessing wrong would quietly turn something into a heading that was not one.

## Should you convert via Word instead?

If your RTF has headings and lists you want kept, there is a simple workaround: open the file in a word processor, save it as .docx, then use [Word to PDF](/word-to-pdf), which keeps headings and lists as well, because .docx carries that structure in a form this product can read reliably. See [how to convert a Word document to PDF without uploading it](/guides/how-to-convert-word-to-pdf-privately).

## Typical uses

- **Plain letters and statements** exported from older software.
- **Notes and drafts** where paragraphs and emphasis are all that matter.
- **Templates and form letters** saved as RTF by legacy systems.
- **Archives** of text documents you want in a more universal format.

## What to check in the result

1. Open the PDF and read the first page. Check that line breaks and paragraph spacing look sensible.
2. Look for any section that used a table or image, which will be missing.
3. If the document had headings that matter, add emphasis back or use the Word route.
4. Check the page count against what you expect.

## Privacy

Legal and financial documents are often the ones stuck in RTF. Because the conversion runs on your device, the document is not sent to any server, which is the point of using a private tool for this job.

## Summary

1. RTF to PDF keeps paragraphs, bold and italic on A4 pages.
2. Headings, lists, tables, images, fonts and colors are not carried over.
3. For headings and lists, save as .docx first and use Word to PDF.
4. The file stays on your device throughout.
