**Short answer:** Open [Scan to PDF](/scan-to-pdf) on a phone, tablet or laptop with a camera, allow camera access, photograph each page in order and save the PDF. No app is needed, and the pictures are not uploaded.

You do not need a scanner or an app to turn paper into a PDF. A phone camera and a browser are enough: photograph each page, put the pictures in order and save them as one file. This guide covers how to do it with LeafLock, how to take pages that come out clean and what the tool does not do for you.

## How to scan with LeafLock

The camera pictures are turned into a PDF by your browser on your device. They are not stored anywhere unless you download the PDF, and they are not uploaded.

1. Open [Scan to PDF](/scan-to-pdf) on a phone, tablet or laptop with a camera.
2. Allow camera access when your browser asks.
3. Photograph each page in order.
4. Retake, reorder or remove pages as needed.
5. Save the PDF.

No app is needed. It works in a modern browser.

## If the camera does not start

Check that camera access is allowed for this site in your browser's settings. If it is blocked, change the permission and reload the page. You can also use **Take photos or choose images** instead: that opens your phone's own camera app or your photo library, and the pictures are then added to the same PDF.

## Taking pages that look good

Nothing straightens or crops the pages for you. Each picture is saved as it was taken, so the quality of the PDF is the quality of your photos. A few habits make a large difference:

- **Lay the page flat** on a plain, contrasting surface, such as a dark desk for white paper.
- **Fill the frame** with the page so no space is wasted.
- **Hold the phone parallel** to the page to avoid skewed edges.
- **Use even light.** Avoid your own shadow, and avoid glare on glossy paper.
- **Hold still** until the picture is taken to avoid blur.

## Retakes and order

While the camera is on, select the retake button under a page and capture it again. You can also reorder or remove pages before saving. Check each page is readable before you save, especially small print, numbers and signatures.

## What the tool does not do

It does not straighten pages, and it does not make the text searchable. Each picture goes in as a picture. To make the words searchable and selectable, run the finished PDF through [OCR PDF](/ocr-pdf). The guide [how to turn a scanned PDF into searchable text](/guides/scanned-pdf-to-searchable-text) walks through it.

## Keeping the file size down

Photo-based PDFs can be large. A ten-page document of full-resolution photos can run to tens of megabytes. If you need to email or upload it, run [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) afterwards; see [how to compress a PDF for email](/guides/compress-pdf-for-email).

## Fixing common problems

- **A page is sideways.** Use [Rotate PDF](/rotate-pdf).
- **Pages are in the wrong order.** Use [Organize PDF](/organize-pdf).
- **A blank page crept in.** Use [Remove pages](/remove-pages).
- **You need a signature on it.** Use [Sign PDF](/sign-pdf); see [how to sign a PDF without printing](/guides/how-to-sign-a-pdf-without-printing).

## Scan or photos-to-PDF?

If you already have photos in your gallery, [JPG to PDF](/jpg-to-pdf) does the joining and lets you choose the page size. Scan to PDF is for capturing as you go. Both end with the same kind of file. See [how to turn photos and images into a PDF](/guides/how-to-turn-photos-into-a-pdf).

## Privacy of what you scan

Scanned documents tend to be sensitive: IDs, bills, medical letters. Because the PDF is built on your device, those pictures are not sent to us. Delete the pictures from your camera roll when you no longer need them, since they remain on your device.

## Summary

1. Allow camera access, photograph pages in order, save as PDF.
2. Good light, a flat page and a filled frame matter more than any setting.
3. No straightening or text recognition; add OCR afterwards if needed.
4. Compress the result before emailing.
