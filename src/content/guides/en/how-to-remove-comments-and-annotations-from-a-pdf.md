**Short answer:** [Remove annotations](/remove-annotations) strips comments, sticky notes, highlights, underlines, strikethroughs, stamps, shapes, drawings, text boxes and their pop-up notes. Form fields are always kept, and links are kept unless you ask for them to go.

Review rounds leave a trail. By the time a PDF has been through two colleagues and a lawyer, it can be covered in sticky notes, highlights, strikethroughs and doodles, some of them embarrassing and some of them confidential. Before it goes to the next audience, you want a clean copy. This guide explains what annotations are, how to strip them and how to be sure the comment text is not still lurking in the file.

## What counts as an annotation

Annotations are markup added on top of a page without changing the page itself. [Remove annotations](/remove-annotations) removes:

- Comments and sticky notes
- Highlights, underlines and strikethroughs
- Stamps
- Shapes
- Freehand drawings
- Text boxes
- The pop-up notes attached to them

## What is kept

- **Form fields** are always kept, because emptying a form is a different job. Use [Flatten PDF](/flatten-pdf) for that.
- **Links** are kept unless you ask for them to go, since a link is navigation rather than markup.

## How to remove annotations with LeafLock

The file is read and rewritten in your browser on your device, and never sent anywhere.

1. Open [Remove annotations](/remove-annotations) and choose your PDF.
2. Choose whether links should go too.
3. Run it and download the clean copy.

## Is the comment text really gone?

Yes. The clean copy is written from scratch and anything no longer referenced is dropped. The comment text and its drawn appearance leave the file, instead of lingering where a reader could still find them. That is the important difference from a tool that merely hides the markup.

## Smaller file too

Usually, and sometimes by a lot. A heavily marked-up document carries a drawn appearance for every comment, and those go too.

## Remove or flatten?

They sound alike but do opposite things.

- **Remove annotations:** the markup disappears from the page entirely.
- **[Flatten PDF](/flatten-pdf):** the markup is painted into the page so it stays visible, but can no longer be edited.

Choose removal for a clean version for outsiders, and flattening for a record of the review. See [how to flatten a PDF](/guides/how-to-flatten-a-pdf).

## What removal does not clean

Annotations are only one place where internal details hide. Also check:

- **Document properties:** author, dates and software. Use [Remove metadata](/remove-metadata); see [how to remove hidden metadata from a PDF](/guides/how-to-remove-hidden-metadata-from-a-pdf).
- **Text on the page:** names, figures and notes typed into the document. Redact them with [Redact PDF](/redact-pdf).
- **Tracked changes from the source document,** if the PDF was exported with them showing.

## A clean-up routine before sending

1. Remove annotations.
2. Remove metadata.
3. Redact any sensitive text.
4. Compare the result with the original using [Compare PDF](/compare-pdf) to see nothing else changed.
5. Compress if needed.

## Check the result

Open the clean copy and scan every page for stray marks. Check pages where a stamp or highlight used to be. Look at the comments panel in your reader and confirm it is empty.

## Summary

1. Comments, highlights, stamps, shapes and drawings are removed.
2. Form fields and, by default, links are kept.
3. The removed content is dropped from the file, not just hidden.
4. Removal and flattening are opposite choices; metadata and page text are separate jobs.
