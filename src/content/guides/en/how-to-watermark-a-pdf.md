**Short answer:** [Add watermark](/add-watermark) puts text, such as DRAFT or CONFIDENTIAL, or a logo image on the pages you choose, with opacity, angle, size and placement you set. For a logo, a PNG with a transparent background works best.

A watermark tells the reader what a document is: a draft, a confidential copy, a sample, or yours. It is also a mild deterrent against passing a file around unnoticed. This guide explains how to add one with text or a logo, how to make it readable without drowning the page and what a watermark can and cannot protect.

## How to add a watermark with LeafLock

The watermark is added in your browser, on your device. The document is not uploaded, which suits confidential files.

1. Open [Add watermark](/add-watermark) and choose your PDF.
2. Choose **Text** or **Image**.
3. Set the opacity, angle, size and placement.
4. Choose which pages get it.
5. Apply and download.

## Text or logo

- **Text** is quick and fits labels such as DRAFT, CONFIDENTIAL, COPY or a recipient's name.
- **Image** suits a logo. A PNG with a transparent background works best, because a solid background would show as a box.

## Making it readable but not intrusive

A good watermark is visible at a glance and does not hide the content.

- **Opacity.** Faint, around a quarter to a third, is usually enough for text. Make it stronger for short words that must not be missed.
- **Angle.** A diagonal watermark is harder to crop out and stands apart from the text lines. Horizontal reads more cleanly.
- **Placement.** Place it once in the center, or repeat it across the page when the document might be cropped or photographed.
- **Size.** Large enough to read, small enough not to cover key figures.

Open the result and check a text-heavy page, a table page and a page with a photo, since a watermark can look fine on one and clash on another.

## Over or under the content?

**Over** is the default and always visible. **Under** puts the watermark behind the text and images, which looks subtler, but it is hidden by scanned pages and solid backgrounds, since those paint over it. For scans, use over.

## Marking only some pages

Choose **Chosen pages** and type the numbers, for example `1, 5-8`. A common pattern is a "CONFIDENTIAL" stamp on every page and a "DRAFT" stamp only on pages still under review.

## How strong is a watermark?

It is drawn into the page content, not added as a comment, so it is not a simple overlay that disappears if someone hides annotations. But someone with PDF editing software could still remove it. A watermark deters casual sharing and marks origin; it is not a lock.

If you need to restrict opening, use [Protect PDF](/protect-pdf). If you need to hide information on the page, that is redaction, not watermarking; see [how to redact a PDF properly](/guides/redact-pdf-properly).

Keep your original without the watermark, so you can produce a new version later.

## Common uses

- **Draft and review copies.** "DRAFT" across the page.
- **Confidential material.** "CONFIDENTIAL" with the recipient's name, so a leaked copy points to its source.
- **Samples and previews.** "SAMPLE" on a product sheet.
- **Branding.** A faint logo on every page of a report.

## Order of operations

1. Edit and finish the content.
2. Add page numbers or Bates numbers if needed.
3. Add the watermark.
4. Compress if needed, then protect or send.

## Summary

1. Text for labels, a transparent PNG for a logo.
2. Keep opacity modest and choose the angle and placement for the document.
3. Use "over" for scanned pages.
4. A watermark marks and deters; it does not secure.
