**Short answer:** Open [JPG to PDF](/jpg-to-pdf), add your images, put them in order and download the PDF; each image becomes one page. JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP work, and JPG and PNG go in unchanged.

Applications for jobs, visas, insurance claims and school forms often want a single PDF, but what you have is a folder of photos. Turning images into a PDF is one of the most common conversions there is. This guide explains how to do it on your device, which formats work, and the choices about page size and quality that decide how the result looks.

## How to make a PDF from images with LeafLock

Your images are turned into a PDF by your browser. They are not uploaded.

1. Open [JPG to PDF](/jpg-to-pdf) and add your images.
2. Put them in the order you want. Each image becomes one page.
3. Choose the page size, see below.
4. Create the PDF and download it.

## Which image formats work

JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP are supported, plus other formats your browser can open. If an image will not open, save it as JPG or PNG from your photo app and add it again. The landing pages [PNG to PDF](/png-to-pdf) and [WebP to PDF](/webp-to-pdf) take you to the same tool.

## Does the quality drop?

JPG and PNG images go into the PDF unchanged. Other formats are converted to JPG or PNG first, which can change them very slightly. So if quality matters, start from JPG or PNG.

## Choosing the page size

By default each page takes the size and shape of its image, so a landscape photo makes a landscape page and a tall screenshot a tall page. That keeps everything unscaled and is best for photos.

If the receiving system expects standard paper, choose **A4** or **US Letter**. Each image is then scaled to fit the page, with an optional margin. That is the better choice for documents, such as a photographed certificate, that will be printed or uploaded to a form expecting a normal page.

## Phone photos and orientation

Phones record which way up a photo was taken in a hidden orientation tag. The tool follows that tag, so sideways pictures are turned upright. If a photo still looks wrong, rotate it in your photo app first, or rotate the page afterwards with [Rotate PDF](/rotate-pdf).

## Keeping the file size sensible

Modern phone photos are several megabytes each. Ten of them in one PDF can easily exceed an upload or email limit. Two ways to keep it down:

- Resize or choose a smaller size in your phone's sharing options before adding them.
- Run the finished PDF through [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf); see [how to compress a PDF for email](/guides/compress-pdf-for-email).

## How many images can I add?

There is no fixed limit. The practical limit is your device's memory, because every image is processed there. For a very large set, build the PDF in two or three parts and join them with [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf).

## Photographing documents

If the images are photos of paper pages, a few habits give better results:

- Lay the page flat on a plain, contrasting surface.
- Fill the frame with the page and keep the phone parallel to it.
- Use even light and avoid shadows from your hand or phone.

You can also capture pages straight from your camera with [Scan to PDF](/scan-to-pdf), or read [how to scan paper documents with your phone camera](/guides/how-to-scan-documents-with-your-phone-camera). To make the text in a photographed page searchable, run [OCR PDF](/ocr-pdf) afterwards.

## Ordering and cleaning up

Check the page order before creating the PDF, since reordering afterwards means another step. If you need to fix it later, [Organize PDF](/organize-pdf) lets you drag pages, rotate them and drop extras.

## Summary

1. One image becomes one page, in the order you add them.
2. JPG and PNG are embedded unchanged; other formats are converted first.
3. Use A4 or Letter for documents, original size for photos.
4. Compress the result if phone photos make it too large to send.
