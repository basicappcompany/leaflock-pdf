**Short answer:** [Word to PDF](/word-to-pdf) reads your `.docx` in the browser and builds a fresh PDF on plain A4 pages in a single typeface, keeping paragraphs, headings, bold and italic text and lists. Tables, images, your fonts and colors, and your page size and margins do not come across, so for a letterhead or a designed layout, export from Word itself.

A Word document is something you can edit; a PDF is something you can send and trust to look the same. Converting from one to the other is a daily job, and it often involves a contract, a CV or a draft you would rather not hand to a stranger's server. This guide explains how to convert a .docx to PDF on your own device, and, just as important, what this conversion does and does not reproduce.

## The honest summary first

[Word to PDF](/word-to-pdf) reads your .docx in the browser and builds a fresh PDF. It does not render the document the way Word does. That has consequences:

**What comes across**

- Paragraphs
- Headings
- Bold and italic text
- Bulleted and numbered lists

laid out on A4 pages.

**What does not come across**

- Tables
- Images
- Your original fonts and colors
- Page size and margins

The PDF is built on plain A4 pages in a single typeface. It is a clean text version of your document, not a faithful copy of its appearance. If the exact layout matters, such as a letterhead, a designed CV or a table-heavy report, this tool is not the right one yet, and exporting to PDF from Word itself is.

## When this tool is the right one

- **Text-led documents.** Letters, notes, essays, meeting minutes and drafts, where the words and their structure matter more than the styling.
- **Sensitive drafts.** Anything you would rather not upload, since the conversion happens on your device.
- **Quick, readable copies.** When you need a PDF to send or archive and a plain layout is fine.

## How to convert with LeafLock

The file is read and the PDF is built in your browser, so it is never uploaded.

1. Open [Word to PDF](/word-to-pdf) and choose a .docx file.
2. Select the convert button.
3. Read the result summary. If your document had tables or images, it tells you how many of each were left out. Nothing is approximated in their place.
4. Download the PDF.

## What the summary tells you

Skipped items are counted and reported, not hidden. If the result says three images and a table were skipped, you know the PDF is incomplete and can decide whether that is acceptable. That is the check to make every time, because a PDF with a missing table can look perfectly tidy.

## Why headings are always bold

A heading style is bold by design in most documents, even when the text itself is not separately marked bold in the file. So headings are always drawn bold here, so they look like headings. If your heading style was deliberately plain, expect it to come out bold.

## Older .doc files

This tool reads .docx, the format current versions of Word save. If you have an older .doc file, open it in Word or another editor and save it as .docx first.

## Alternatives for documents that need full fidelity

- Use your word processor's own **Export as PDF** or **Print to PDF** command. It has the original fonts, tables and images, and it still runs on your device.
- For spreadsheets, see [how to convert Excel and CSV to PDF](/guides/how-to-convert-excel-and-csv-to-pdf).
- For rich text files, [RTF to PDF](/rtf-to-pdf) handles paragraphs, bold and italic only.

## After converting

- Check the page count and read the first and last page.
- Add page numbers with [Add page numbers](/add-page-numbers) if the document will be printed.
- Compress it if it needs to be emailed: [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf).
- To go the other way, from PDF back to an editable document, see [how to convert a PDF to Word](/guides/how-to-convert-pdf-to-word-and-what-survives).

## Summary

1. Conversion happens on your device; the file is not uploaded.
2. Text, headings, bold, italic and lists come across; tables, images, fonts, colors and margins do not.
3. The result tells you how many tables and images were skipped.
4. For exact layout, export from Word itself.
