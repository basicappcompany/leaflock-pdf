**Short answer:** Open [PDF to JPG](/pdf-to-jpg), choose JPG or PNG and a resolution, then download the pages one by one or as a ZIP. Use JPG for photographic pages, PNG for text and diagrams, and 72 dpi when the images are only for screens.

Sometimes a PDF is the wrong container: you want a page for a slide, a thumbnail for a website, a picture to post in a chat or an image to paste into a design. Converting pages to JPG or PNG gives you ordinary image files. This guide covers how, and how to choose the format and resolution that suit the job.

## How to convert with LeafLock

Each page is drawn as an image by your browser, on your device. The PDF is not sent anywhere.

1. Open [PDF to JPG](/pdf-to-jpg) and choose your file.
2. Pick the format, JPG or PNG, and the resolution.
3. Choose all pages, or **Chosen pages** and type the numbers, such as `1-3, 5`.
4. Convert, then download the images one by one or select **Download all as ZIP**.

[PDF to PNG](/pdf-to-png) opens the same tool with PNG already selected.

## JPG or PNG?

- **JPG** makes smaller files and suits pages with photos and scans. It handles gradients well. Fine text and thin lines can pick up slight blurring around the edges.
- **PNG** keeps text and lines sharper because it does not lose detail, but the files are larger. It is the better choice for pages that are mostly text, charts or diagrams.

A quick rule: photographic pages as JPG, text and diagrams as PNG.

## Choosing the resolution

Resolution is measured in dots per inch (dpi). It controls how many pixels each page gets.

- **72 dpi** is for screens: previews, thumbnails and quick sharing.
- **150 dpi** is a standard all-rounder, good for on-screen reading and ordinary printing.
- **300 dpi** is for print, and for pages where small text must stay readable when zoomed.

Higher resolution means larger images and more memory use. Very large pages may come out at a lower resolution than you asked for, to stay within browser limits, so check the size of a result if you need an exact pixel count.

## Converting only some pages

Most of the time you want one or two pages, not all 80. Choose **Chosen pages** and type them. This is faster and uses less memory than converting everything and throwing most of it away.

## Why long documents can be a problem

Every image is made in your device's memory. A long document at 300 dpi can be too much, especially on a phone. If the job stalls or fails, convert fewer pages at a time, or lower the resolution.

## What you lose when a page becomes an image

An image is a picture of a page. The text is no longer text: you cannot select it, search it or copy it, and links stop working. If you need the words, use [PDF to Text](/pdf-to-text), or keep the PDF. If the PDF contains photos you want back in their original quality, [Extract images](/extract-images-from-pdf) pulls them out as the files they were, rather than as a picture of the page; see [how to extract images from a PDF](/guides/how-to-extract-images-from-a-pdf).

## Typical jobs

- **A page for a presentation.** PNG at 150 dpi.
- **A thumbnail for a website.** JPG at 72 dpi.
- **Sharing one page in a chat app** that does not take PDFs. JPG at 150 dpi.
- **Archiving a scan as images.** PNG at 300 dpi, then compress the set.
- **Sending a single page.** [Extract pages](/extract-pages) first, if the receiver can take a PDF.

## Summary

1. Choose JPG for photographic pages, PNG for text and diagrams.
2. 72 dpi for screens, 150 as standard, 300 for print.
3. Convert chosen pages only to save memory.
4. Images are pictures: text is no longer selectable or searchable.
