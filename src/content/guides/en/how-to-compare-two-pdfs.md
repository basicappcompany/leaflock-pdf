**Short answer:** Open [Compare PDF](/compare-pdf), choose the original and the revised file, and read the text changes first, then the visual overlay. Pages are matched by position, page 1 with page 1, so a page added or removed in one version shifts everything after it.

Two versions of a contract arrive an hour apart and nobody says what changed. A supplier sends a "revised" price list. A colleague re-exports a report and swears only one number is different. Reading both side by side is slow and unreliable, because the eye skips exactly the small changes that matter. This guide shows how to compare two PDFs on your device and how to read the result.

## What the comparison shows

[Compare PDF](/compare-pdf) gives you two views of the same pair of files.

- **Text changes.** Words added or removed, page by page.
- **A visual overlay** that highlights areas that look different, such as images or layout.
- **Side by side** pages, so you can look at both at once.

## How to compare with LeafLock

Both PDFs are opened and compared in your browser, and neither is uploaded. That matters for drafts of agreements and other documents you would not want on a stranger's server.

1. Open [Compare PDF](/compare-pdf).
2. Choose the original and the revised version.
3. Run the comparison.
4. Read the text changes first, then check the visual overlay.

Compare PDF is a Practice tool.

## How pages are matched

Page 1 is compared with page 1, page 2 with page 2 and so on. If a page was added or removed, the pages after it shift, and the comparison may show many changes even though the content is the same, just moved.

To avoid that, remove or add the page in one version to line them up first, using [Remove pages](/remove-pages), or compare the sections separately after splitting with [Split PDF](/split-pdf).

## Scanned documents

The text comparison needs selectable text, which scans do not have. The visual overlay still shows where scanned pages differ. To compare the words of a scan, run [OCR PDF](/ocr-pdf) on both first; see [how to turn a scanned PDF into searchable text](/guides/scanned-pdf-to-searchable-text).

## Formatting changes

The text comparison only looks at the words. A change in font, color or styling shows up only in the visual overlay. So read both views: a changed clause may not be a changed word, if only emphasis moved.

## Reading a result well

1. **Start with the text changes.** They tell you what was added and removed.
2. **Check the overlay for the rest:** images, tables and layout.
3. **Confirm anything important in the pages themselves,** since an automated comparison can miss a changed digit inside an image.
4. **Note the page shift** if the counts differ.

## Good uses

- **Contract revisions:** what did the other side change?
- **Price lists and terms:** what moved between versions?
- **Reports re-exported from source:** did anything change that should not have?
- **Before and after of a clean-up:** did removing metadata or annotations alter the content? See [how to remove hidden metadata from a PDF](/guides/how-to-remove-hidden-metadata-from-a-pdf).
- **Redaction check:** compare the redacted file against the original to confirm only intended areas changed; see [how to redact a PDF properly](/guides/redact-pdf-properly).

## Limits

A comparison finds differences; it does not judge them. A reordered paragraph and a deleted paragraph can look similar. Always read the flagged sections in context.

## Summary

1. Compare shows word changes, a visual overlay and side-by-side pages.
2. Pages are matched by number, so an added page shifts everything after it.
3. Scans need OCR for text comparison; the overlay still works.
4. Formatting changes appear only in the overlay.
